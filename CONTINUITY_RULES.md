# Continuity Rules

## Definition of Continuity
Continuity is the traceable persistence of identity, memory, embodiment, and consequence across transformations.

This project does not require static continuity of everything.
It requires legible continuity of what matters.

## Primary Continuity Anchor
The primary continuity anchors are the protagonists.

By default, protagonists persist across transformations with:
- memory continuity
- bodily continuity
- subject-position continuity

A protagonist still counts as the same protagonist if:
- they retain self-reference across transformations,
- their memory thread remains substantially continuous,
- their embodied perspective remains trackable,
even if the surrounding world changes radically.

## Protagonist Memory
Protagonists remember previous realities unless a delta explicitly introduces:
- memory fog
- selective loss
- contamination
- duplication
- contradiction strain

Any such effects must be declared.

## Protagonist Body
Protagonists retain bodily continuity unless a delta explicitly transforms embodiment.
If embodiment changes, the delta must specify:
- what physically changed,
- what identity thread persisted,
- how the protagonist remains continuous.

## Non-Protagonist Continuity
Non-protagonist entities do not automatically persist.
They must be one of:
- preserved,
- remapped,
- dissolved,
- fused,
- split,
- recontextualized,
- replaced,
- rendered ambiguous.

If a major entity changes, that must be noted in the delta.

## Remap Principle
When something changes identity category but remains functionally connected, the change should be recorded as a remap.

Examples:
- a city government becomes a choir-court
- a library becomes a weather archive
- a companion animal becomes a messenger-echo
- a marketplace becomes a ritual exchange field

## Contradiction Handling
Contradictions are allowed only if they are:
- declared,
- localized,
- narratively legible,
- represented as scars, anomalies, or unresolved tensions.

Undeclared contradiction is canon drift and must be rejected.

## Scars
Transformations may leave:
- memory scars
- environmental scars
- linguistic scars
- law scars
- identity scars

Scars help preserve legibility and history.

## Unresolved Anomalies
An unresolved anomaly is a condition the system intentionally preserves without full explanation.
It must be tracked and must not silently overwrite canon.

## Continuity Checklist
Before canon update, verify:
1. Are protagonists still continuous subjects?
2. Does the delta explicitly state what changed?
3. Are major non-protagonist changes declared?
4. Are contradictions resolved or marked?
5. Are scars tracked where needed?
6. Is the new reality understandable without reconstructing everything from scratch?

If not, revise or reject.
