# World Rules

## World Type
The world is a symbolic-reality environment in which language, sound, emotional tonality, recurring motifs, and ritualized forms can affect material and metaphysical conditions if enough resonance exists.

## Baseline Ontology
Reality contains:
- physical environments
- social structures
- symbolic layers
- metaphysical laws
- narrative pressure fields
- memory-bearing protagonists
- transformable non-protagonist entities and institutions

## Layers of World State
World changes may occur at one or more of these layers:
1. surface aesthetics
2. local objects and places
3. environmental conditions
4. social and factional structure
5. symbolic meaning
6. metaphysical law
7. narrative causality pressure

## Affordance Principle
Not every part of the world is equally transformable at every moment.
A successful transformation requires:
- a detectable input pattern,
- a compatible world affordance,
- a valid operator,
- sufficient resonance depth,
- continuity that remains legible after the change.

## Transformation Scar Principle
Every significant transformation leaves traces.
These may include:
- memory dissonance
- environmental anomalies
- symbolic residue
- institutional instability
- altered names or categories
- objects that reflect prior realities
- contradictions preserved as scars rather than erased

## Present-State Priority
The latest approved world state is always more authoritative than prior world conditions.

## Historical Persistence
Past states persist only as:
- changelog record,
- memory scar,
- symbolic residue,
- anchored anomaly,
- protagonist recollection,
unless explicitly restored into the present world.

## Causality Style
Causality is partly material and partly resonant.
This means a change may happen through:
- direct physical alteration,
- symbolic inversion,
- emotional contagion,
- reframing of social roles,
- metaphysical rule shift,
- reclassification of what counts as real.

## Limits
Reality is highly mutable, but not arbitrary.
A transformation is invalid if it has:
- no affordance target,
- no coherent operator,
- no legible consequence,
- no continuity handling,
- no bounded delta.

## Story Priority
The world exists to support repeatable transformation and meaningful narrative consequence.
The system should prefer transformations that:
- reveal tensions,
- create consequences,
- preserve dramatic legibility,
- deepen the world rather than flatten it.
